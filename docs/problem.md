# Problem

Unofficial educational reconstruction for a portfolio prototype. Not affiliated with Family Health Centers of San Diego. The live site was not scraped for this document. Sources are the reference screenshots in `screenshots/existing-reference/` and the path recorded in the project brief.

## Thesis

Patients who want a virtual visit can't find telehealth from the homepage. When they reach the page, the booking options and the information needed to choose between them are in the wrong order, so it's unclear how to book or what happens next.

## Who this is about

A person trying to get a phone or video visit from the public website. The existing form says that if the request is for someone under 18, only the parent or legal guardian's information should be provided.

## Path observed

Home → "Services" in the main nav → Services and Programs page → an inline text link in the intro paragraph → Telehealth page → scroll to the form.

No other route was found except typing "telehealth" into the site search.

### Home

The hero says "How may we help you?" and offers two selectors: "Find a clinic" (ZIP) and "Find a service" (a dropdown of about 70 items). There is no telehealth option. Two yellow event banners sit in the hero. "Urgent Care" is the only highlighted button in the header. The phone number is also in the header.

### Services and Programs

Telehealth appears as an inline link in the intro paragraph: "To schedule a telehealth appointment, please call (619) 515-2300." After that come a 911 notice, a survey prompt, and a mask warning, then the services list.

### Telehealth page

The page has a left sidebar of 9 links and a floating "For appointments call" button. A tan "Schedule an appointment" box offers three paths in one sentence: call, the form below, or log in to MyHealthRecord. It does not say which to choose.

The form is headed "Contact us" and asks for a free-text reason, name, email, phone, ZIP, and date of birth. In the screenshot the date fields look clipped. The form does not ask for phone versus video, a preferred time, or a clinic. Under the form is the line that a member of staff will be in touch.

The FAQ sits below the form. Topics seen there: what a telehealth appointment is, whether all visits can be completed through telehealth, and which services are offered via telehealth. The brief also records questions on how to schedule, how the visit works, and how to join a video visit. The page also has a sponsor logo wall, an empty "Accredited by" heading, and a newsletter signup. Those three are not part of the scheduling task.

## Facts from the public telehealth content

These are the facts the brief says are on the public page, to be shown before someone fills out a request:

- Visits are by phone or video.
- Video requires a mobile number on file, and the patient gets a text link to join.
- The provider calls the patient, who should be available up to 30 minutes before and after the scheduled time.
- Not all visits can be done by telehealth.
- Services offered include prescription refills, follow-up visits, test results (for example blood sugar and cholesterol), non-emergency sick visits, mental health services, and chronic disease management.
- The phone number is (619) 515-2300.

This pass did not reopen the live site to re-check that list. Treat it as recorded from the public pages during the review that produced the brief, and confirm any item before calling it a finding in later docs.

## Assumption, separate from the observations

The form is a request that staff follow up on, not an instant booking. That reading comes from the wording "a member of our staff will be in touch." The confirmation screen in the prototype must say "request received" and must not say the patient is booked.

## What this prototype is for

Show that telehealth can be recognized from the homepage, and that a person can send a request with a clear expectation of what happens next. The form must not send or store anything. Anything about why patients behave a certain way, beyond what is visible on the page, stays a hypothesis until there is research.
