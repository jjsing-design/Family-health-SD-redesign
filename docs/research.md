# Research

The problem statement and audit were accepted on October 17, 2026. This file separates what was observed from assumptions and hypotheses. No patients have been interviewed.

## Evidence

Observed on the public site, from the reference screenshots and the path in the brief:

- The path to telehealth is Home → Services → an inline link in the Services and Programs intro → the Telehealth page → scroll to the form.
- No other route was found except typing "telehealth" into site search.
- The home hero offers Find a clinic (ZIP) and Find a service (a long dropdown). It does not offer telehealth. Two event banners sit in the hero. Urgent Care is the only highlighted header button. The phone number is in the header.
- On Services and Programs, telehealth is an inline link that tells the reader to call (619) 515-2300, followed by a 911 notice, a survey prompt, a mask warning, and the services list.
- The telehealth page has a left sidebar of 9 links, a floating "For appointments call" button, and a tan box that offers call, the form, and MyHealthRecord in one sentence.
- The form is headed "Contact us." It asks for a free-text reason, name, email, phone, ZIP, and date of birth. The date fields look clipped. It does not ask for phone versus video, a preferred time, or a clinic.
- The FAQ sits below the form.
- The page also has a sponsor wall, an empty "Accredited by" heading, and a newsletter signup.
- Public telehealth facts recorded from the page: visits are by phone or video; video needs a mobile number on file and a texted join link; the provider calls, and the patient should be available up to 30 minutes before and after the scheduled time; not all visits can be done by telehealth; services include prescription refills, follow-up visits, test results, non-emergency sick visits, mental health, and chronic disease management; the phone number is (619) 515-2300.

## Assumptions

- The form is a request that staff follow up on, not an instant booking. This comes from the wording "a member of our staff will be in touch."

## Hypotheses

These are the Problem, User impact, and Expected outcome columns from the accepted audit. They are not findings.

- Telehealth is easy to miss because it is not a visible home option, so many people will not find it. A Virtual visit entry on the hero would make it one click from home.
- Event banners and a single highlighted Urgent Care button split attention from the primary task. Moving events lower and having one primary action would clarify the hierarchy.
- The inline telehealth link is easy to miss, and it says "call" while the telehealth page offers more than a call. A direct entry from home, with compact notices, would be recognized faster.
- Three booking paths in one sentence make the patient evaluate the options alone, which may lead to wrong turns or abandonment. One primary path, with the others labeled as alternatives, would reduce wrong turns.
- "Contact us" under "Schedule an appointment" does not match the outcome, so patients may think they are booked. Naming it "Request a telehealth visit" and stating what happens next would set an accurate expectation.
- The form leaves out visit type and preferred times, so staff must call back to collect them. Asking for phone or video and preferred days and times would make the first request more useful.
- Eligibility sits below the form, so fit is decided before the form is filled, which produces uninformed requests. Putting eligibility and expectations first would lead to better-informed requests.
- Clipped date fields, the sidebar, and the floating call button add friction. Larger inputs and removing the sidebar from the flow would make the form easier to complete.
- Text over photographs on the Services page may have low contrast. This still needs a contrast check before it is stated as a fact.
